Data analysis by Pratiti Toppo, based on 96,211 delivered orders from Olist, a Brazilian online marketplace, from January 2017 to August 2018

Olist more than doubled revenue. Almost nobody came back.

Revenue grew 143% year on year, but monthly growth stalled in 2018. Using SQL on eight linked tables, this analysis shows why: 97% of customers bought only once, and late deliveries were destroying the first impression.

    Growth flattened at about 1M BRL a month in 2018

    Revenue climbed steadily through 2017 and peaked on Black Friday, up 54% on October. In 2018 it moved sideways. Switch between revenue, orders and average order value to see that the plateau came from order volume, not basket size.

    Only 3% of customers ever placed a second order

    Each row follows the customers who first bought in that month. Fewer than 1% returned in any of the next six months, and newer cohorts did no better than older ones. Growth depended entirely on finding new buyers.

    Percent of each first-purchase cohort that ordered again, by months after the first order.

    One-time big spenders hold 27.5% of revenue

    Customers were scored on recency and spend in quintiles, plus whether they ever reordered. The "at risk high-value" group spent heavily once and has not returned in over a year. They already trusted Olist with a large order, which makes them the best target for a win-back campaign.

    Truly loyal customers, recent and repeat, are just 1.3% of the base.

    Reviews collapse the moment an order arrives late

    Orders that arrive early or on time average above 4 stars. Orders more than a week late average 1.7 stars, and nearly 4 in 5 get a 1 or 2 star review. Customers judge the delivery promise, not the speed.

    Late deliveries by customer state, states with 1,000+ orders. CE, BA and RJ are late 2 to 3 times as often as São Paulo.

    Revenue is spread across many categories

    The top 10 categories make up 62% of revenue and none exceeds 10%, so the marketplace does not depend on a single product line.

    What Olist should do next

    1. Make retention the top priority. Post-purchase emails, a second-order coupon and category-based recommendations, because 97% of customers never return.
    2. Win back the at-risk high-value customers. About 13,800 one-time buyers who hold 27.5% of revenue.
    3. Fix delivery promises in CE, BA and RJ. Improve carriers or set realistic dates there, since reviews fall only when an order is late.
    4. Reach out proactively on late orders with an apology or a small voucher, before the customer leaves a review.
    5. Plan stock and marketing around Black Friday, the strongest month of the year.

    How this was built

    Data
    Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist (Kaggle): 99,441 orders across orders, customers, items, payments, reviews, products, sellers and category tables.
    Scope
    Delivered orders, January 2017 to August 2018. Revenue is item price plus freight, in Brazilian reais (BRL). Customers are counted by customer_unique_id, because customer_id changes with every order.
    SQL
    DuckDB with multi-table joins, CTEs and window functions (LAG, RANK, NTILE, FIRST_VALUE) for growth, cohorts, RFM and delivery analysis.
    Python
    pandas, Matplotlib and Seaborn in a Jupyter notebook; one script reruns every query and refreshes this page.
    Limitations
    No website or cart data, so conversion and cart abandonment can't be measured. The link between late delivery and lost repeat purchases is suggested by the data, not proven.
